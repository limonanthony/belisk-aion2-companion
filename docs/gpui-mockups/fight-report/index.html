End-of-fight report for boss fights TASK-15.1: approved 2026-10-07 with the crown, now in approved/report.html, the summary states of approved/meter.html and approved/history.html; this page is kept as history

  • After a boss fight, a button opens a full report of that fight: for every player the damage and DPS, the healing, the damage taken, and the deaths (who died, when in the fight, what killed them). A click on a player opens everything they did: damage per skill, how many critical hits and every other hit type, how long each buff was up (m:ss), their heals per skill, the damage they took by source, their deaths.
  • Boss fights only. No boss health graph and no hit-by-hit timeline: the deaths are a list with the time into the fight, not a timeline.
  • Drawn on the approved overlay language: the 520 px window of the detail window, the meter's bars and rank badges (the less-rounded ends), Barlow Semi Condensed with tabular figures, the class icons and skill icons from crates/app/assets/, the opacity and content scale controls. Each page draws every state over three game scenes.
  • Game names are real data (Basilus the False in Fallen Guardian's City, Tyrant Tassin, Superbia, the class and monster skills); which monster used which skill, the players and every figure are invented.

C, one table, every figure chosen

One row per player with Damage, DPS, Healing, Damage taken and Deaths side by side; a click on a column header sorts by it and the bars and ranks follow. The deaths are listed under the table. Damage taken by source and heals by skill are in the player details. The user: "the table shows more infos without clicking".

Player details chosen: inside the report

The approved detail window's layout, extended: Healing, Damage taken and Deaths join the key figures; the hit types show counts and rates ("665 41.9%"); the skill table gains a Crits column; four tabs, Damage, Buffs (uptime as m:ss, "4:01"), Healing (per skill) and Damage taken (by monster skill). Opens inside the report from a player's row or their line under Deaths, with "‹ Report" to go back.

Opening the report chosen

The approved boss summary card gets "Open the fight report", full width under the skills, on the final card and on the provisional "Fight ended, may resume" card. History gets a report button on boss records only, so a report can be opened again after the summary is gone.

The boss icon before the boss's name in History chosen: the crown

Boss records get a small icon before the boss's name so they stand out from mob records (the two mob records here have none, and no report button). Both are Lucide icons, 12 px in hp, the boss gauge's colour. Chosen on 2026-10-07: the crown ("yeah use a crown"), as recommended: the report already draws the skull for deaths (the Deaths column, the deaths list, a player's death plate), so a skull on a record could read as "someone died in this fight"; the crown says "boss" and means nothing else in the meter. The "Boss icon" control on Opening the report switches every History state.

Decided 2026-10-07

  1. Report layout: C, one table. "The table shows more infos without clicking." A (tabs) and B (one scrolling report) were not chosen; they stay below as history.
  2. The button on the summary card: "Open the fight report", full width under the skills. "Report" beside Copy and an icon in the header were not chosen.
  3. Also from History: a report button on each boss record, never on mob records; boss records also get an icon before the name: the crown (chosen with the final approval). "Only inside an opened record" was not chosen.
  4. Player details open inside the report, from a player's row or their line under Deaths, with "‹ Report" to go back. The detail window was not chosen.
  5. Uptime as m:ss, "4:01", like the fight time.
  6. Hit types as count and rate, "665 41.9%".
  7. The report is also offered from the provisional summary ("Fight ended, may resume").
  8. Damage taken by source: per player only, in the player details.
  9. Sorting in C: Damage first; the sort is kept while the window is open.
  10. Figure alignment: plates left, tables right. "It looked better when it was aligned start + table aligned end." The key-figure and hit-type plates keep label above value at the left of each cell, as the approved detail window does; every table puts its figures and headers at the right of each column (decimal points and the K and M suffixes line up). All start, All center and All end were not chosen; align.html keeps all four behind its "Alignment" control as history.

Why C

  • The user: "the table shows more infos without clicking". The request is one look at "how did everyone do and what happened": damage, heals, damage taken and deaths at once. C answers it on one screen with no click; A hides three answers behind tabs, B spreads them over a long scroll and repeats every name three times.
  • The comparisons players make after a wipe or a slow kill run across measures (the healer's output beside the tank's damage taken, a death beside low damage); only C puts them on the same row.
  • It stays short on a game screen: six players and a death fit in about 400 px. The detailed breakdowns (by skill, by source, uptime) live one click away in the player details, where they have room.
  • It reuses what is approved: the meter's rows, the approved sorting pattern (active header in text with an arrow, the others underlined), the history and detail windows' header and figures.

Not decided by these drawings

  • Which effects count as heals (shields, life drain such as Blood Absorption) is checked on a recording with a healer before TASK-15.2 settles it; the drawings show direct heals, heals over time and self-heals. Overheal is not shown.
  • The killing blow ("last hit") assumes the meter can tie a death to the hit before it; TASK-15.2 confirms what the packets give.
  • Fights saved before this change open with Damage, skills and buffs, and say that healing, damage taken and deaths were not recorded (drawn on every page).
